About this platform

Built to help remote workers stay alert, stay safe, and work with confidence.

What this is

SE Aware is a free, publicly accessible learning platform about social engineering, the family of attacks that target people rather than software. Instead of breaking encryption or exploiting a bug, a social engineer persuades you to hand over a password, approve a payment, or install something. The technique is old. What has changed is how convincing it has become.

The platform organises that subject into six modules. Five cover a specific attack type: phishing, spear phishing, vishing, smishing, and pretexting. Each one follows the same structure, so you learn what the attack is, how it unfolds step by step, what a realistic example looks like, which red flags to watch for, and what to do if it has already happened to you. The sixth module collects the everyday habits that make all five attacks harder to land.

Who it is for

Remote workers, freelancers, virtual assistants, and anyone else who earns a living through a laptop and an internet connection. That group is targeted at roughly the same rate as office employees, but rarely has access to the structured security awareness training that larger organisations run internally. Most material that does exist publicly is written for IT professionals or scattered across a dozen unrelated websites.

Everything here is written for a reader with no cybersecurity background. Where a technical term is unavoidable, it is explained the first time it appears.

Why we built it

We surveyed 54 remote professionals, freelancers, and hybrid workers about their actual experiences. 85.2% had received a suspicious email claiming to be from a bank or company. 92.6% had received a prize or raffle scam text. 53.7% admitted clicking a link that only afterwards seemed suspicious.

Yet self-assessed understanding of social engineering types averaged only 3.35 out of 5, the lowest score in the whole survey. 72.2% said they were not aware of any publicly available website about this subject at all, and every single respondent agreed that remote workers need more cybersecurity awareness training. This platform is our response to that gap.

What this platform does not do

This is the part worth reading carefully. SE Aware is an educational resource, not a security product. Specifically, it does not:

  • Scan, inspect, or analyse your emails, links, QR codes, SMS messages, or attachments
  • Detect threats in real time or monitor you while you browse or work
  • Block, filter, quarantine, or remove anything malicious
  • Replace antivirus software, a VPN, endpoint protection, or your employer's security controls
  • Provide professional cybersecurity consultation, incident response, or legal advice
  • Cover cybersecurity domains outside social engineering. Malware analysis, digital forensics, penetration testing, network monitoring, and vulnerability assessment are all out of scope

The AI assistant available on every page is bound by the same limits. It explains attack patterns and points you toward the right module. If you are dealing with an active incident, where money has already moved or accounts are already compromised, contact your bank, your employer's security team, and the appropriate authorities directly. The assistant is not a substitute for any of them.

How your privacy is handled

There is no account system, no login, and no database behind the free tier. We do not ask for your name, your email, or anything else, because we have nowhere to put it and no reason to want it. Nothing you type into the assistant is stored after you close the page.

Quiz scores are held inside your own browser tab so the results page can show them to you, and they never leave your device. Close the tab and they are gone. That is a deliberate design decision, not a missing feature.

About the project

SE Aware was built as a Capstone 1 project for MO-IT200D1 at Mapúa Malayan Digital College, in partial fulfilment of the requirements for a Bachelor of Science in Information Technology major in Network and Cybersecurity.

The educational content is drawn from peer-reviewed cybersecurity literature and current industry threat reporting. Social engineering tactics change quickly, so material is written to describe patterns and underlying psychology rather than any single campaign that will be obsolete in a year.

Knowledge today. Protection always. A safer tomorrow.
